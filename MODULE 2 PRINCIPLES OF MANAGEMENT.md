`// Classical School of Management \\`
   Focuses on efficiency, productivity, structure, and formal organization.

  3 branches
  1. Scientific Management
    Founder: Frederick Winslow Taylor
    There is "one best way" to perform a job through scientific study.
    - Replaced rule-of-thumb work methods with proven methds
     > Principles
        Science, not rule of thumb
        Harmony, not discord
        Cooperation, not individualism
        Maximum outout
        Development of workers
 
     > Contribution
        Time study
        Motion study
        Work measurement
        Standardization
        Differential wage system

  2. Administrative Management
      Founder: Henry Fayol
      Focuses on structuring an entire organization to improve efficiency and coordination.
      
      Shifts the `focus from individual worker tasks` (like Scientific Management) to the `overall organizational structure`, managerial behaviors, and administrative systems

      > 5 Functions of Management
       1. Planning
       2. Organizing
       3. Commanding
       4. Coordinating
       5. Controlling

      > 14 Principles of Management
       1. Division of Work
       2. Authority and responsibility
       3. Discipline
       4. Unity of Command
       5. Unity of Direction
       6. Subordination of Individual interests
       7. Remuneration
       8. Centralization
       9. Scalar Chain
       10. Order
       11. Equity
       12. Stability of Tenure of Personnel
       13. Initiative
       14. Espirit de Corps

  3. Bureaucratic Management
      Founder: Max Weber
      Organizations should operate according to rational rules and formal structures.
      - Clear hierarchy
      - Formal rules
      - Merit-based recruitment
      - Impersonal relationships



`// Classical School of Management \\`
  Developed as reaction against the mechanical approach of the Classical School.
  Focus on human behavior, psychology, and social relations in the workplace.
  - Human relations
  - Informal organization
  
  > Major Pillars
     1. The Human Relations Movement
         Elton Mayo
         Workers are social beings with emotional needs, not just economic cogs.
         Introduced the idea that treating workers with respect, their well-being

     2. The Behavioral Science Approach
         Maslow, McGregor, Herzberg
         Applies psychology and sociology to understand why employees behave the way they do.



`// Behavioral School of Management \\`
  Focuses on understanding human behavior in organizations.
  > Major Contributors:
    Abraham Maslow, Douglas McGregor
    Chris Argyris, Renis Likert

  1. Maslow's Need Hierarchy Theory
      Abraham Maslow, 1943
      Human actions are motivated by a psychological drive to `satisfy` five basic categories of `needs`
      
  > 5 Levels
    1. Physiological Needs:
        Crucial biological requirements needed for human survival.
        - Food, water, air, sleep, shelter, and clothing.

    2. Safety Needs:
        The drive for predictability, order, and control over one's life.
        - Job security, safe working conditions, health insurance, and savings plans.

    3. Social Needs:
        The human need for emotional relationships and interpersonal connectivity.
        - Collaborative team cultures, workplace friendships, and supportive managers.

    4. Esteem Needs:
        The desire for respect, social status, and personal appreciation.
        - Job promotions, award ceremonies, praise from executives, and prestigious job titles.

    5. Self-Actualization Needs:
        Realization of a person's full potential, self-fulfillment, and personal growth.
        - Challenging assignments, creative freedom, autonomy in decision-making, and opportunities for continuous learning.

   
   2. McGregor's Theory X and Theory Y    
       Douglas McGregor, 1960
       In book `The Human Side of Enterprise`.

       Manager's leadership style is shaped entirely by  assumptions about worker behavior and attitude.

       > Theory X
          Assumes employees:
          Dislike work
          Avoid responsibility
          Need strict supervision

          Management Style:
           Authoritarian & Micro-managing, 
           Heavy Reliance on financial rewards and punishment
       
       > Theory Y 
          Assumes employees:
          Enjoy work
          Accept responsibility
          Self motivated

          Management Style:
           Participative & Decentralized
           Alignment of Goals

`/// Systems Theory/School \\\`
  Ludwig von Bertalanffy
  Understanding how a business functions
  An org is a system of interrelated parts working together.

  > Core System Components

  
    ┌─────────────┐     ┌──────────────┐     ┌─────────────┐
    │   INPUTS    │ ──> │TRANSFORMATION│ ──> │   OUTPUTS   │
    │ Raw, Labor  │     │  Processes   │     │ Goods, ROI  │
    └─────────────┘     └──────────────┘     └─────────────┘
         ▲                                       │
         └──────────────  FEEDBACK  ─────────────┘

   > Inputs:
      Raw data, user commands, or external triggers fed into the system.
   > Transformation Process:
      The internal programs, calculations, workflows, and algorithms that manipulate the input.
   > Outputs:
      The final result generated, such as automated invoices, data reports, or shipped products.
   > Feedback Loop:
      System logs, error messages, or user reviews used to fix bugs and optimize future processing

   Helps managers understand the organization as a whole.
 
   `Key System Characteristics Analysts Must Manage`
    1. Interdependence
    2. Entropy
    3. Decomposition

 `//// Principles of scientific management and their application in libraries \\\\`

`// Time Study and Motion Study \\`
  Developed during the Scientific Management era

  > Motion Study
     The "How"
     Frank and Lillian Gilbreth
     To find the one best way to perform a job safely and effortlessly.
     To improve the method of work.
     Video cameras, charts, and Therbligs are used
     Reduces worker fatigue and waste.

* Therbligs: Breaking down a job into fundamental body motions

  > Time Study
     The "How Long"
     Frederick Winslow Taylor
     To establish a fair day's work and measure productivity.
     To calculate the standard time for work.
     Stopwatches and time-recording sheets.
     Increases speed and helps budget labor.

 `// PERT \\`
  Program Evaluation and Review Technique
  1958
  By United States Navy
  For Polaris Missile Program

  Used to plan, schedule, and control projects where `activity durations are uncertain`

  - Event-oriented technique
  - Used when activity times are uncertain.
  - Suitable for research and development projects.
  - Uses probability estimates
  - Does not emphasize cost control

  PERT uses `3 time estimates`:
   1. Optimistic Time (O)
       Minimum possible time required
   2. Most Likely Time (M)
       Most probable time required
   3. Pessimistic Time (P)
       Maximum possible time required

    Estimated Time Formula:       
        𝑇𝑒 = 𝑂+4𝑀+𝑃

                6
    Te = Expected time
    O = Optimistic time
    M = Most likely time
    P = Pessimistic time
    


`/// CPM \\\`
 Critical Path Method
 1950s
 Dupont & Remington Rand

  Used for planning and controlling projects where activity `durations are known and predictable`

 Longest path through a project network and determines the minimum time required to complete the project.
 
 > Activities on the critical path:
    Zero Float (Slack)
    No delay tolerance
 
 - Activity-oriented technique
 - Assumes known activity durations
 - For construction and production projects
 - Emphasizes time and cost optimization
 - Identifies critical path and optimize project time & cost





`/// Decision Tables \\\`
  For analyzing and presenting complex decision situations involving multiple conditions and actions.

  Helps managers choose appropriate actions based on different combinations of conditions.

  > Major Components
     Part	            Description
     Condition Stub	   List of conditions or factors
     Condition Entries	Possible values of conditions
     Action Stub	      List of actions to be performed
     Action Entries	   Actions corresponding to conditions

  > Types
    1. Limited-Entry Decision Table
        Conditions have only two values: Yes/No, True/False
        Eg: Book: Available → Yes/No
    2. Extended-Entry Decision Table
        Conditions can have multiple values.
        Membership Category:Student, Faculty, Research Scholar
    3. Mixed-Entry Decision Table
        Contains both limited and extended entries.






`\\\\ Methods of Financial Estimation ////`

> Historical Method:
   Forecasts future needs using past expenditure trends.
   Eg: Estimate using past expenditure

> Comparative Method:
   Benchmarks budget requirements against similar peer institutions.
   Eg: Compare with similar university libraries

> Unit Cost Method:
   Calculates budgets by multiplying tasks by single-unit costs.
   Eg:Cost per book processed


> Incremental Method:
   Modifies the past budget by a flat percentage
	 Eg: Increase previous year's book budget by 10%

> Zero-Based Budgeting:
   Starts from zero; requires every expense to be justified.
	Eg: Justify every database subscription

> Performance Budgeting:
   Allocates funds based on measurable operational outputs.
	Eg: Funds based on circulation and usage statistics

> Programme Budgeting:
   Groups expenditures around long-term strategic projects.
	Eg: Budget for automation project

> Formula Budgeting:
	Budget based on student population




`//// Budgeting Techniques \\\\`

> Line-Item Budgeting/Traditional Method
   Expenditure is listed under specific items or heads.
   Books, Salaries, Furniture etc

> Zero-Based Budgeting:
   Every budget period starts from zero, & every expenditure must be justified.
   By Peter A. Pyhrr
   Eg: Journal subs, DB renewal, Tech investment

> Performance Budgeting:
   Allocation is linked to activities, outputs, performance.
   Cost per book catalogued, Cost per user served

> Programme Budgeting:
   Funds are allocated according to programmes and projects.
   Digitization program, Automation program etc

> Formula Budgeting:
   Funds are allocated according to a predetermined formula.
   Budget based on: Number of students, Collection size etc

> PPBS Planning Programming Budgeting System
   Integrates: Planning, Programming, Budgeting.
   For large academic and research libraries.





`//// Quality Management \\\\`
  4 Pillars
   1. Quality Planning
       Identifying the quality standards relevant to the project or service and determining how to satisfy them.
   2. Quality Control
       The operational techniques used to inspect, test, and verify that specific outputs meet quality standards 
   3. Quality Assurance
       The planned, systematic workplace processes designed to ensure that the operational system will inherently prevent defects before they happen.
   4. Quality Improvement
       Taking deliberate actions to increase the effectiveness and efficiency of processes

*  ^ Juran's quality trilogy




> Six Sigma:
   A data-driven methodology that uses statistical analysis to eliminate process defects, aiming for no more than 3.4 defects per million opportunities.

`/// SERVQUAL \\\`
  Service Quality Model
  1985
  By A. Parasuraman, Valarie A. Zeithaml, Leornad L. Berry

  `𝑆𝑄 = 𝑃 − 𝐸`

  For assessing service quality by measuring the `gap between customer expectations and perceptions`.

  - Measure service quality.
  - Identify service gaps.
  - Improve customer satisfaction.
  - Assess organizational performance.
  - Support continuous quality improvement.

  > 5 Dimensions of SERVQUAL
     `TRRAE`
     1. Tangibles:
         Physical facilities, equipment, appearance of personnel.
     2. Reliability:
         Ability to perform the promised service accurately and dependably.
     3. Responsivness:
         Willingness to help users and provide prompt service.
     4. Assurance:
         Knowledge, competence, courtesy, and trustworthiness of staff.
     5. Empathy:
         Providing individualized attention and care to users.

  > 5 Organizational Gaps
    - Gap 1: The Management Perception Gap
    - Gap 2: The Service Standards Gap
    - Gap 3: The Service Delivery Gap
    - Gap 4: The Market COmmunication Gap
    - Gap 5: The Service Quality Gap

  > LibQUAL+
     Modified into LibQUAL+
     By Association of Research Libraries (ARL)
     Evaluates lib service quality across 3 dimensions
      1. Affect of Service (Staff Empathy)
      2. Information Control (Resource Availabilty)
      3. Library as Place (Physical Environment)


 By committees like ISO/TC 46

`/// ISO 9000 series of Standards \\\`
  By International Organization for Standardization.
  Family of `international standards` related to Quality Management Systems (QMS).
  Provide `guidelines and requirements` for organizations.
  
  - Fundamentals of quality management
  - Vocabulary and terminology
  - Basic concepts of QMS




  `\\ Major Standards //` 
   * ISO 9001
      Quality Management Systems - Requirements
      Outlines the exact `criteria` an organization must fulfill `to achieve official certification`.
      Most Important standard for orgs

      `Only standard` in the series that can be `audited and certified`.

      `What requirements must be met`

      > Key Requirements
         Quality policy, Quality objectives, Documentation, Process control, Performance evaluation, Continous improvement

      > Library Application
         1. Standard procedures
         2. Consistent service quality
         3. User satisfaction measurement

   * ISO 9004
      Guidance for sustained organizational success and performance improvement.
      
      Focus: `How can quality be improved beyond minimum requirements?`

   * ISO 19011
      Provides guidelines for auditing management systems.
      A guidance standard, not a requirements standard.
      Focus: Internal external audits, Auditor competence
      Useful in `conducting quality audits` in libraries and information centres
