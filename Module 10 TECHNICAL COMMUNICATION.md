

`/// Types of Writing \\\`

  > Narrative Writing 
     To tell a story or describe events.
     Chronological order
     Beginning, middle, and end
     Eg: Novels, Short stories, Biographies, Autobiographies

  > Descriptive Writing
     To describe a person, place, object, or event.
     Detailed description
     Sensory language
     Creates vivid images
     Eg: Travel writing, Descriptions in books, Nature writing

  > Expository Writing
     To explain, inform, or clarify.
     Fact-based, Logical presentation, Objective approach
     Eg: Textbooks, Encyclopedias, Research explanations

  > Persuasive Writing
     To convince readers to accept a viewpoint.
     Arguments and evidence
     Logical reasoning
     Opinion-oriented
     Eg: Editorials, Advertisements, Opinion articles

  > Academic Writing
     To communicate scholarly knowledge.
     Formal language,
     Evidence-based
     Proper citation
     Eg: Research papers, Dissertations, Theses, Journal articles

  > Technical Writing
     To communicate scientific, technical, or professional information clearly and accurately.
   > Eg:
      Manuals, Technical reports, User guides, Standard operating procedures, Project reports,Scientific documentation



`/// Technical Writing \\\`
  To communicate scientific, technical, or professional information clearly and accurately.
  - Provide instructions and guidance.
  - Explain procedures and processes.

  > Principles
     Accuracy, Conciseness, Completeness, Objectivity, Consistency, Simplicity, Logical, Readability

  > Examples
     Manuals, Technical reports, User guides, Standard operating procedures, Project reports,Scientific documentation





 `/// Abstracts \\\` 

  1. Indicative Abstracts
      Aka Descriptive Abstract
      Describes the scope and content of a document without presenting detailed results or conclusions.
      It tells `what the document is about.`

      - Brief
      - No detailed findings
      - No numerical data

  2. Informative abstract
      `Summarizes` all important aspects of a document, including `objectives, methodology, results, and conclusions`.

      It tells `what was done and what was found.`

      - Detailed
      - Contains findings & conclusions
      - May include numerical results

  3. Critical abstracts
      `Summarizes` the document but also `evaluates` its `quality, reliability, and significance`.

      - Includes criticism and evaluation.
      - Analytical in nature
      - Prepared by subject experts

  4. Slanted Abstarcts
      `Prepared` for a `specific group of users` and `emphasizes aspects of the document` relevant to that audience.

      - User-oriented
      - Selective emphasis
      - Customized scientists

  5. Structured Abstracts
      `Presents` information under `specific headings`.
      > Common Headings:
         Objective, Methodology, Results, Conclusion
      > Used in:
         Medical Journals, Scientific journals, Research DBs








`/// Style Manuals \\\`

> Chicago Manual of Style (CMS)
   By University of Chicago Press
   1906
   > Commonly Used:
      History, Humanities, Arts, Publishing Industry

   > Citation Systems
   1. Notes & Biblio systems
       Uses Footnotes, Endnotes, Bibliography
       Used in History, Literature, Humanities

   2. Author-Date System
       Flexible style
       Detailed docu system
       Supports both notes & author-date citation
       `Used in Sciences, Social Science`
       Eg: (Ranganathan 1931, 25)


> APA Style
   American Psychological Association Style
   By American Psychological Association
   1929
   Current ed. : 7th
   > Commonly Used:
      Psychology, Education, Sociology, Management, SS

   > Citation Methods
   1. Author-Date System
       - Emphasis on publication date.
       - Widely used in social sciences.
       - Arranged alphabetically.
       In-text Citation
         (Ranganathan, 1931)
       Direct Quote
         (Ranganathan, 1931, p. 25)
      > Example:
         Ranganathan, S. R. (1931). Five laws of library science. Madras Library Association.




> MLA Style
   Modern Language Association
   By Modern Language Association
   1951
   Current Edition: 9th
   > Commonly Used:
      Literature, Languages, Humanities, Cultural Studies

   > Citation Method
      Author-Page System
       Emphasizes authorship
       Uses "Works Cited" instead of "References."

`/// Copy Editing vs Proofreading \\\`


| Aspect          | Copy Editing                         | Proofreading                        |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------- |
| Stage           | Before typesetting                   | After typesetting                   |
| Purpose         | Improve content and language         | Detect final errors                 |
| Scope           | Broad                                | Limited                             |
| Focus           | Grammar, style, consistency, clarity | Typographical and formatting errors |
| Changes Allowed | Major and minor corrections          | Mostly minor corrections            |
| Performed On    | Manuscript                           | Proofs (final version)              |
