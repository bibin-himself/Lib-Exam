

`/// Sources of Information \\\`

`// Primary Sources \\`
   Original information
   1st Publication
   Most Current
   > Eg:
      Research Article, Case studies, Experimental reports
      Patent, Thesis, Dissertation, Project reports, Conference papers, Technical reports, Lab reports, Standards.Specifications, Census reports, White papers, Sppeches, Interviews, Raw datsets, court judgements etc

`// Secondary Sources \\`
  Derived from primary sources
  Organized & Structured
  Facilitates literature search
  Save user's time
  > Eg:
  1. Indexing Sources: Index Medicus, SCI, SSCI, Indian Science Abstracts
  2. Abstracting Sources: MEDLARS, AGRIS, INIS, Chemical Abstracts, INSPEC, LISA
  3. Review of Progress
  4. Citation Databases: Scopus, Web of Science
  5. Current Contents
  6. Treaties
  7. Monographs
  8. Textbooks


`// Tertiary Sources \\`
  Summarized information
  Ready reference
  Easy consultation
  General overview
  > Eg:
     Encyclopaedias, Dictionaries, Bibliographies, Guides, Directories, Almanacs, Gazetteers, Handbooks, Library Catalogues, Yearbooks etc
     






`///// Ready Reference Sources \\\\\\`
  Provide quick, concise, and factual information.
  Eg: Dictionaries, encyclopedias, directories, yearbooks etc


 `/// Dictionaries \\\`
   Provide meanings of words.
   Alphabetical arrangement.
   Ready reference source.
   Word-oriented source.
   Provides linguistic information.

   > Types:
   1. General Dictionaries
       > Cover words used in general language.
       Eg: Oxford English Dictionary, Merriam-Webster Dictionary, Chambers Dictionary, Collins English Dic

   2. Subject Dictionaries
       > Cover terminology of a specific subject.
       Eg: Dorland's Medical Dictionary, Black's Law Dictionary, Harrod's Librarians' Glossary and Reference Book etc

   3. Monolingual Dictionaries
       > Word and meaning are given in the same language.
       Eg: Oxford English Dictionary, Merriam-Webster Dictionary, Chambers Dictionary

   4. Bilingual Dictionary
       > Provide equivalent words in two languages.
       Eg: English–Hindi Dictionary, English–Malayalam Dictionary, English–French Dictionary, Hindi–English Dictionary

   5. Multilingual Dictionaries
      > Provide equivalents in more than two languages.
      Eg: UNESCO Multilingual Thesaurus, Multilingual Technical Dictionary, International Electrotechnical Vocabulary (IEV)

   6. Historical Dictionary
      > Historical development and changes in word meanings.
      Eg: Oxford English Dictionary (OED), Dictionary of Old English

   7. Etymological Dictionary
      > Explains the origin and derivation of words.
      Eg: Oxford Dictionary of English Etymology, Chambers Dictionary of Etymology

   8. Pronouncing Dictionary
       > Provides pronunciation of words.
       Eg: Daniel Jones English Pronouncing Dictionary,Longman Pronunciation Dictionary

   9. Learner's Dictionary
      > Prepared specifically for language learners.
      Eg: Oxford Advanced Learner's Dictionary, Longman Dictionary of Contemporary English, Cambridge Advanced Learner's Dictionary

   10. Dictionary of Synonyms (Thesaurus)
       > Provides words with similar meanings.
       Eg: Roget's Thesaurus, Oxford Thesaurus of English

   11. Dictionary of Antonyms
       > Provides opposite words.
       Eg: Oxford Dictionary of Synonyms and Antonyms, Merriam-Webster Dictionary of Synonyms and Antonyms

   12. Dictionary of Quotations
       > Contains famous quotations.
       Eg: Oxford Dictionary of Quotations, Bartlett's Familiar Quotations

   13. Biographical Dictionary
       > Provides information about notable persons.
       Eg: Dictionary of National Biography (DNB), Who's Who

   14. Geographical Dictionary (Gazetteer)
       > Provides information about places.
       Eg: Gazetteer of India, Columbia Gazetteer of the World

   15. Electronic / Online Dictionary
       > Available through computers and the Internet.
       Eg: Oxford Learner's Dictionaries, Cambridge Dictionary, Merriam-Webster Dictionary
    



 `/// Encyclopaedias \\\`
   Explains subjects and topics.
   Info on all branches of knowledge or a particular subject
   Arranged alphabetically.
   Provide reliable info on a subject.
   Includes cross-references.
   Written by subject experts.

   - Meaning: Circle of Learning (Enkyklios Paideia)
   - Most Famous Encyclopaedia: Encyclopaedia Britannica
   - First Published: 1768 (Scotland) EB

   > Types

   1. General Encyclopaedias
      > Cover all branches of knowledge.
      Broad coverage
      Multi-disciplinary
      Eg: Encyclopaedia Britannica, Encyclopedia Americana, World Book Encyclopedia
   
   2. Subject Encyclopaedias
      > Cover a particular discipline or subject.
      Eg: Encyclopedia of Library and Information Science, Encyclopedia of Medicine, Encyclopedia of Education, Encyclopedia of Computer Science

   3. National Encyclopaedias
      > Focus on a particular country.
      Eg: Bharatiya Vidya Bhavan's Encyclopedia, Banglapedia

   4. Children's Encyclopaedias
      > Prepared specifically for children.
      Eg: Children's Britannica, World Book Encyclopedia

   5. Electronic Encyclopaedias
      > Available in digital form.
      Eg: Britannica Online, Wikipedia

`Based on Arrangement`
    
   6. Alphabetical Encyclopaedia
      > Entries arranged alphabetically.
      Eg: Encyclopaedia Britannica

   7. Systematic (Classified) Encyclopaedia
      > Entries arranged according to subject classes.
      Eg: McGraw-Hill Encyclopedia of Science & Technology, The Worldmark Encyclopedia of Cultures and Daily Life
  



`/// Yearbooks \\\`
  Summary of important events, developments, statistics, facts, and achievements of a `particular year`.
  Published annually
  Factual and statistical in nature.
  Arranged systematically by subject.

  > Types
  1. General Yearbooks
     > Cover events and developments from all fields.
     Eg:
     The Statesman's Yearbook - 1864, Palgrave Macmillan
     World Almanac and Book of Facts

  2. National Yearbooks
     > Focus on a particular country.
     Eg: India Year Book - Publications Division

  3. Subject Yearbooks
     > Cover developments in a particular subject.
     Eg: Yearbook of Agriculture, Yearbook of Education, Statistical Yearbook
  
  4. Institutional Yearbooks
     > Published by organizations and institutions.
     Eg: University Yearbooks, Corporate Yearbooks, Annual Reports

`/// Information Gateways \\\`
  Provides organized access to quality-controlled information resources available on the Internet
  > Bridge between users and information resources
  Structured collection of selected and evaluated Internet resources

  - Quality controlled
  - Subject-Oriented
  - Metadata-Based
  - Reliable Sources
  - Improve information retrieval.

  > Types
  1. Subject Gateways
      Cover a specific discipline
      > SOSIG
      By University of Bristol
      Subject: Social Sciences
      > OMNI
      By University of Nottingham
      Subject: Medicine & Health Sciences
      > EEVL
      By Heriot-Watt University
      Subject: Engineering

  2. Institutional Gateways
      Developed by institutions for their users.
      Eg: University Portals, Institutional Knowledge Portals

  3. Commercial Gateways
      Managed by commercial organizations.
      Eg: Corporate Information Portals, Business Information Gateways





`/// Table of Contents \\\`
  List of the contents of a document showing the titles of chapters, sections, articles, or parts
  Along with their page numbers.
  
  - Appears at the beginning.
  - Provides a systematic overview of the publication
  - Information Retrieval
  - `Roadmap to the document.`
        


`/// Preprints \\\`
  Research paper shared publicly before undergoing formal peer review by a journal.
  Allows researchers to disseminate research findings rapidly and receive feedback
  Major component of Open Science and Scholarly Communication.

  - Not Peer Reviewed
  - Rapid Dissemination
  - Increasing visibility of research

  Eg: arXiv, bioRxiv, medRxiv, SSRN, Research Square, ChemRxiv
