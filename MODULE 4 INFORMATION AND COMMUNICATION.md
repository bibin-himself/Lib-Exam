`// Aristotle's Model //`
  384–322 BC
  By Aristotle

  `Speaker → Speech → Audience` or
  `Speaker → Message → Listener`
     
  One-way communication
  Focus on public speaking
  No feedback
  Suitable for speeches & lectures

  Eg: Teacher → Lecture → Students
     

`// Lasswell's Model \\`
  1948
  Harold D. Lasswell

  `Who - Says What - In Which Channel - To Whom - With What Effect`

  `Sender → Message → Channel → Receiver → Effect`

  Linear Model
  Emphasizes effect of communication
  Widely used in mass communication

  Eg: News Channel → News → Television → Viewers → Awareness


`// Shannon and Weaver Model \\`
  1949
  Claude E. Shannon & Warren Weaver
  Known as the `Mathematical Model of Communication`

  Information Source
        ↓
   Transmitter
        ↓
   Signal
        ↓
   Channel
        ↓
   Receiver
        ↓
   Destination

* Noise may interfere in the channel

  Inroduced the concept of `Noise`
  Basis of Information Theory
  Useful in telecommunications & info systems

  Eg: Telephone communication affected by network disturbance



`// Berlo's SMCR Model \\`
  1960
  By David K. Berlo
  
  `Source → Message → Channel → Receiver`

  Emphasis communication Skills
  Human-centered model
  Widely used in education







`// Schramm's Model \\`
  1954
  Wilbur Schramm

  Sender ↔ Receiver
         ↕
      Feedback

  Two-way communication.
  Introduced `Feedback`.
  Emphasized Field of Experience.

  > Field of Experience
     Communication is effective when sender and receiver share common experiences and understanding.
     Eg: Teacher and student interaction
   

`// Osgood–Schramm Circular Model \\`
  1954
  Charles E. Osgood
  Wilbur Schramm
  
  Encoder
     ↓
  Interpreter
     ↓
  Decoder
     ↺

  Circular process.
  Sender and receiver continuously exchange roles.
  Feedback is continuous.
  Eg: Face-to-face conversation.


`// Newcomb's ABX Model \\`
  1953
  Theodore M. Newcomb

  A ↔ B     A = Sender B = Receiver
   \ /
    X       X = Topic or Object

  Focuses on social relationships.
  Communication maintains balance between participants.




`// Westley–MacLean Model \\`
  1957
  Brice H. Westley
  Malcolm S. MacLean Jr.

  Designed for mass communication.
  Includes gatekeepers and feedback.
  Explains news communication process.




`// Dance's Helical Model \\`
  1967
  Frank E. X. Dance
  Helix (Spiral) shape

  Shows communication as an evolving process.

  Communication grows continuously.
  Influenced by past experiences.
  Dynamic process.

`/// Scientific Communication - Trends \\\`
 1. E-Publishing
     Publishing scholarly content in electronic form
     Eg: E-journals, E-books, Online conference proceedings

     Faster publication
     Low cost
     Global accessibility
     Full-text searching



 2. Open Access Movement
     Free, unrestricted online access to scholarly literature.

     > Major Declarations
       1. Budapest Open Access Initiative (BOAI) (2002)
       2. Bethesda Statement on Open Access Publishing
       3. Berlin Declaration on Open Access

     > Types
       1. Gold OA
           Published directly in OA journals
       2. Green OA
           Self-archiving in repositories
       3. Diamond OA
           No charges for authors or readers
       Eg: Directory of Open Access Journal (DOAJ)
           PubMed Central




 3. Institutional Repositories (IRs)
     Digital archives maintained by institutions for preserving and disseminating scholarly output.

     Long-term preservation
     Increased visibility
     Supports Green OA

     > Softwares
       1. DSpace:
           MIT Libraries & Hewlett-Packard
           Most widely used repository software
       2. EPrints:
           University of Southampton
           First widely adopted OA repository software
       3. Greenstone:
           University of Waikato, SW & UNESCO
           To build and distribute digital library collections
      
      Indian Eg:
       IRs of IITs, IISc, and universities.




 4. Digital Libraries
     Organized digi collections accessible through networks.
     Eg: National Digital Library of India : IIT Khargpur
          Internet Archive



 5. Online Databases and Information Retrieval Systems
     Computerized systems for storage and retrieval of scientific information.
     Eg: PubMed, AGRIS, INIS



 6. Open Science   
     Making all aspects of research openly accessible.
     Transparency
     Reproducibility
     Collaboration

     > Components
        Open Access, Open Data, Open Source Software, Open Peer Review, Open Educational 



 7. Preprint Servers
     Repos where researchers share early, unpublished versions of their scientific manuscripts before formal peer review and journal publication
     Immediate dissemination
     Early feedback
     Increased visibility
     Eg:
      > arXiv:
         1991
         Repo for scientific preprints in physics, math, computer science, and related fields.
         Paul Ginsparg; managed by Cornell University.

      > bioRxiv:
         2013
         Online archive and distribution server for preprints in the biological sciences.
         Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory (CSHL) - Dev & Oper
       
      > medRxiv:
         2019
         Repo for complete but unpublished preprints in medical, clinical, and health sciences.
          CSHL, Yale University, and BMJ (British Medical Journal).



 8. Digital Object Identifier (DOI)
      A unique and permanent identifier assigned to digital documents.
      Persistent linking
      Citation tracking
      Easy retrieval

      DOI `remains unchanged` even if the webpage location changes.

      > Registration Agency : Crossref
      Eg: 10.1000/xyz123 (Prefix/Suffix)




 10. Scholarly Social Networking
       Platforms where researchers share publications and collaborate.
       Similar to SM but
       Focus entirely on scientific output, academic visibility, and professional collaboration.

       > Services:
          Profile creation
          Paper sharing
          Citation tracking
          Discussion forums
      
       Eg: ResearchGate, Academia.edu



 11. Metrics
      1. Bibliometrics
          Study of books, articles, citations.
          Indicators:
           Citation count
           h-index
           Impact Factor

      2. Scientometrics
          Measurement of scientific productivity and growth.
      
      3. Altmetrics
          Measures online attention.
          Sources: SM, Blogs, News articles, Downloads






`//// OPEN ACCESS MOVEMENT \\\\`
  Free, immediate, online availability of scholarly literature
  Without financial, legal, or technical barriers
  While respecting author attribution and copyright.

  Ensure publicly funded`research freely available` to everyone.

| Year | Initiative                                                        | Importance                                 |
| ---- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| 1991 | arXiv launched by Paul Ginsparg at Los Alamos National Laboratory | First major preprint repository            |
| 2002 | Budapest Open Access Initiative                                   | Formal beginning of OA movement            |
| 2003 | Bethesda Statement on Open Access Publishing                      | Defined OA publishing principles           |
| 2003 | Berlin Declaration on Open Access                                 | International support for OA               |
| 2018 | Plan S                                                            | Requires publicly funded research to be OA |

> Types
  1. Gold Open Access
      Research is published directly in an OA journal.
      Immediate free access.
      Publisher provides access.

      Eg: Public Library of Science
           2001
           Harold Varmus, Patrick Brown and Michael Eisen
           Publishes fully OA journals such as PLOS ONE.

      May involve Article Processing Charges (APCs).

   2. Green Open Access
       Authors deposit a copy of their work in an institutional or subject repository.

       Self-archiving.
       May involve embargo periods.

       Eg: arXiv, IR: DSpace, EPrints

   3. Diamond (Platinum) Open Access
       No fee for authors and no fee for readers.

       Eg: university-supported OA journals.

       Community or institution funded.
       Completely free publishing model.

   4. Hybrid Open Access
       Subscription journals offer OA for individual articles after payment of APCs.
       Eg: Many commercial publishers provide hybrid options.

`/// Directory of Open Access Journals \\\`
  2003
  Launched By Lund University Libraries
  Managing Org: Infrastructure Services for Open Access
  Hq: UK

  Leading directory of peer-reviewed Open Access journals.
  Acts as a quality-controlled index of scholarly journals.
  Provide free access to research articles.

  > DOAJ Seal
     Awarded to journals that follow best practices in OA publishing.
     Indicates a high-quality OA journal.
     Requirements include:
      Permanent article identifiers (e.g., DOI)
      Long-term digital preservation
      Machine-readable licensing information
      Open licensing policies
  
  Supported by OAPEN Foundation

`// Information Society \\`
> Fritz Machlup
   Introduced the concept of the `Knowledge/Info Industry`.
   In his book The Production and Distribution of Knowledge in the United States (1962).

> Peter Drucker
   Popularized the concept of the `Knowledge Worker`.
   Emphasized knowledge as a major economic resource.

> Daniel Bell
   Developed the `concept of Post-Industrial Society`.
   Explained the shift from industrial production to information and services.

> Yoneji Masuda
   Considered one of the principal theorists of the `Information Society`.
   Authored The Information Society as Post-Industrial Society (1980).

> Manuel Castells
   Developed the concept of the `Network Society`.
   Explained the role of information networks in modern society.

`// Right to Information Act \\`
  2005, Enforcement: 12 October 2005
  Ministry:
   Department of Personnel and Training (DoPT)
   Promotes transparency and citizens' access to government information.
     
   Applicable To:
   Entire India (except the former special provisions applicable to Jammu & Kashmir before constitutional changes).

   > Key Sections
      Section 2(f):     Information
      Section 2(h):     Public Authority
      Section 6:        RTI Application
      Section 8:        Exemptions
      Section 19:       Appeals
      Section 20:       Penalties

   > Information Bodies
      Central Information Commission
      State Information Commissions

   > Time Limits
      Normal Cases:            30 days
      Through APIO:            35 days
      Life or Liberty Cases:   48 hrs
      Third Party Information: 40 days

   > Information Exempted from Disclosure
      National Security
      Foreign Relations
      Cabinet Papers
      Trade Secrets
      Personal Information
      Information Forbidden by Courts

   > Appeals Under RTI
      1st Appeal
       Filed before the senior officer of the same public authority.
       Time Limit: Within 30 days.

      2nd Appeal
       Filed before CIC or SIC.
       Time Limit: Within 90 days.

   > Penalties
      PIO may be penalized for:
       Refusal to receive application.
       Delay in providing information.
       Providing false information.
       Destroying information.
      250/day, Maximum: 25000

   > RTI Amendment Act, 2019
      Generated debate regarding the independence of Information Commissions.
