
`//// Universe of Subjects \\\\`
  Total field of human knowledge from which subjects are derived for classification and indexing.


`//// Structure \\\\`
 1. Basic Subjects
     Fundamental Disciplines of knowledge
     Independant subjects

     Eg: Maths, Physics, Chemistry etc

 2. Compound Subjects
     Formed by adding 1 or more factes to a basic subject
     Eg: Library Automation, Agricultural Economics etc

     Basic Subject + Facet(s) = Compound Subject

 3. Complex Subjects
     Formed by interaction of 2 or more subjects
     Eg: Physics in Medicine, Economics of Education etc

 4. Multidisciplinary/Interdisciplinary SUbjects
     Draw knowledge from several disciplines
     Eg: Environmental Studies, Data Science etc

`/// Modes of Formation of Subjects \\\`

> LLFFDDDAC
  
  1. Lamination
      Formation of compound subject
      By adding facets(isolates) to a basic subject

      `Basic Subject + Facet(s) = Compound Subject`

      Eg: Agriculture in India
          Human Body - Anatomy
          Library Automation

  2. Loose Assemblage
      Bringing together separate subjects to study their relationship.
      Subjects remain independant
      `No new discipline` is created

      `Subject A + Subject B = Study of Relationship/Application`

      Eg: Statistics for Librarians, Physics for engineers

  3. Fusion
      Merging separate subjects to create a new discipline.
      New Primary Basic Subject

      `Primary Basic Subject A + Primary Basic Subject B = New Interdisciplinary Subject`

      Eg: Astrophysics, Biochemistry, Geophysics

  4. Fision
      Splitting of a broad subject into smaller subjects

      `Broad Subject → Specialized Subjects`

      Eg: Botany and Zoology, Microeconomics and Macroeconomics, Algebra and Geometry

    
  5. Distillation
      Extracting common concepts from several disciplines to create a new subject.

      `Common Concepts/Methods from Multiple Subjects = New Independent Discipline`

      Eg: Computer Science, Microbiology, Statistics

  6. Dissection
      Dividing a broad subject into coordinate parts of equal rank.

      `Subject → Coordinate Subdivisions`

      Eg: History, Geography, and Civics (Social Studies), 
          Botany, Zoology, and Microbiology (Biology)

  7. Denudation
      Successive narrowing from broad to specific

      `Broad Subject → Narrower Subject → More Specific Subject`

      Eg: Science - Biology - Zoology - Entomology 
          Philosophy - Logic - Deductive Logic

  8. Agglomeration
      Grouping independent subjects under a broad umbrella area.

      `Subject A + Subject B + Subject C + ... = Broad Subject Group`

      Eg: Social Science, Biomedical Science, Humanities

  9. Cluster
      Grouping subjects around a single person, place, or phenomenon.

      `Subjects/Literature Related to X = Cluster Around X`

      Eg: Gandhian Studies, Climate Change Literature

`// TYPES OF LIBRARY CLASSIFICATION \\`  

  1. Purely Enumerative Scheme
      Subjects are listed in advance.
      Little or no synthesis.
      Compound subjects are already provided in schedules.
      Difficult to accommodate new subjects.

      `Classification Number = Pre-enumerated Subject`

      Eg: LCC

      Easy to use,
      Simpleshelf arrangement
      Suitable for large collection

  2. Almost Enumerative Scheme
      Enumerates most subjects and additionally provides `common isolates`.
      Limited synthesis possible.
      Uses `standard subdivisions/common auxiliaries`.

      Eg: DDC

      `Enumerated Subject + Common Isolates = Class Number`
        History of India + Standard subdivision

  3. Almost Faceted Scheme
      Enumerated subjects plus schedules of `common isolates and special isolates`.

      Greater synthesis than DDC
      `Auxillary tables`
      Special facets for discipline

      `Enumerated Subject + Common Isolates + Special Isolates = Compound Subject`

      Eg: UDC, BC

  4. Rigidly Faceted Scheme
      Facets and their sequence are predetermined for each subject

      Fixed facet formula and citation order
      Strong apply of facet analysis
      Less flexibility

      Basis: `PMEST` ,;:.'

      Eg: CC early editions

      `Basic Subject + PMEST Facets (in fixed order) = Compound Subject`
        Agriculture : Rice : Cultivation : India : 2024

  5. Almost Freely Faceted Scheme
      Uses Fundamental Categories and connecting symbols, reducing rigidity.

      Facet sequence more flexible.
      Uses PMEST concepts.
      Not completely bound by fixed formula.

      `Basic Subject + Relevant Facets + Connecting Symbols = Compound Subject`

      Eg: CC 4, 5, 6 editions

  6. Fully Freely Faceted Scheme
      No predetermined facet formula or or citation order
      Maximum Flexibility

      largely a theoretical ideal

      `Subject Analysis → Determine Facets → Determine Citation Order → Construct Number`

      
       
      


`/// Mapping of the Universe of Subjects \\\`
  Representing entire body of knowledge and relationships among subjects in a classification scheme.

 `Past Subjects + Present Subjects + Future Subjects = Universe of Subjects`

 > 3 Major Mapping Patterns

                Universe of Subjects
                         │
     ┌───────────────────┼───────────────────┐
     │                   │                   │
 Linear Mapping     Tree Mapping        Web Mapping
     │                   │                   │
 Shelf Order        Hierarchical      Multidimensional
     │                   │                   │
   DDC/LCC          Fission                Fusion
                    Dissection             Distillation
                    Denudation             Loose Assemblage
                    

    Linear Mapping  = Single sequence
    Tree Mapping    = Hierarchical structure
    Web Mapping     = Network of relationships


   1. Linear Mapping
       Subjects arranged in a single sequence.
       `A → B → C → D → E`
       Eg: DDC (000 → 100 → 200 → 300 → 400)

   2. Tree Mapping (Hierarchial)
       Knowledge represented as a hierarchy.
        Science
        ├─ Physics
        ├─ Chemistry
        └─ Biology
       Weak in representing interdisciplinary subjects
       Fission

   3. Web Mapping (Network/Multidimensional)
       Knowledge represented as interconnected nodes.
        Physics ───── Mathematics
            │              │
            │              │
        Engineering ─ Computer Science
       Eg: CC, Thesauri, Semantic web, Ontologies
       Fusion, Distillation, Loose Assemblage

      Division
         ↓
